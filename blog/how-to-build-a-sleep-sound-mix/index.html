How to build a sleep sound mix

By Cason Clark, maker of La-Lo-Ka. The method works in any mixer app; examples use La-Lo-Ka's sound names.

A good sleep mix is boring in the best way: steady, warm, and with nothing that jumps out. Three layers is usually plenty.

Step 1: Pick a bed (60–80%)

The bed is the steady sound that does most of the masking. Steady rain is ideal: tent rain, light rain or a wide "3D" rain. If street noise is the problem, put brown noise here instead of rain.

Step 2: Add a texture (20–40%)

Texture makes the mix feel like a place. Quiet fire, ocean waves or a box fan under the rain. Keep it clearly quieter than the bed.

Step 3: Add a far accent (10–25%)

Thunder works best far away. Rolling thunder at a low level adds depth; sharp crackling thunder near the top can wake light sleepers, so keep it low or skip it.

Step 4: Levels and timer

  • Set the phone volume first, then balance layers. Quieter than you think: you should be able to hear a person talking over it.
  • Use a sleep timer of 30–60 minutes with a fade-out, or all night if the mix masks noise you need gone.
  • Save the mix so tomorrow is one tap.
